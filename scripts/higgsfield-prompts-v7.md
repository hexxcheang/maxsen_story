# Higgsfield prompts: "The Name" (pixel art, v7)

Two ways to use these:
- **Option A:** paste the single brief into Higgsfield's AI assistant/chat and let it build the whole video.
- **Option B:** paste each block into the video generator one at a time (10s, 9:16), then join them in editing.

---

## Option A: one brief for the Higgsfield assistant

```
Make a 60-second narrated faceless story video, 9:16 vertical, with burned-in subtitles.

STYLE (lock for the whole video): cinematic 16-bit pixel art, like a modern premium indie game. Warm, restrained palette: deep navy shadows, amber and soft gold light, muted ivory walls. Rich lighting with soft glow and bloom, crisp pixel edges, slow gentle camera moves. Quiet, elegant, nostalgic mood. No faces visible on any person: hands, silhouettes or figures from behind only. No text, no logos, no UI in the visuals.

CHARACTERS: an older man (the father, an electrical engineer), always seen from behind or as hands only. A young man (the son), always seen from behind or as hands only.

NARRATOR: calm, warm, unhurried male voice, slow pace with pauses. MUSIC: sparse soft piano, no chiptune, no beat.

6 blocks of 10 seconds, 5 shots of about 2 seconds each:

BLOCK 1. Narration: "My father was an electrical engineer. He built things to solve problems. He called his company Maxsen."
Shots: a Singapore HDB apartment block at night with one window glowing amber, slow push in / a cluttered workbench under one desk lamp, an older man's hands soldering / extreme close-up of a green circuit board with a tiny solder spark / a small handwritten name card propped against an old toolbox / the older man from behind holding up a small gadget blinking a soft light.

BLOCK 2. Narration: "I studied engineering too. And I kept his name. But I wanted to build something different. Something beautiful."
Shots: the same workbench years later in daylight, younger hands opening the same old toolbox / engineering sketches and drafting tools on the desk / a pencil drawing an elegant square wall switch on paper / young hands placing the old circuit board next to a sleek new switch faceplate / a young man from behind at the bench as the lamp clicks on.

BLOCK 3. Narration: "The things you touch every day should be worth touching."
Shots: a plain dull white plastic light switch on a grey wall, flat lifeless light / hard cut to a square switch plate covered in tiny crystals catching a moving light, pixel sparkles scattering / a fingertip turning the ridged metal ring of a round dimmer with an iridescent mother-of-pearl face / a slow slide along a matte laminate switch plate to a thin line of crystals at its edge / a dark room where one touch key on a switch glows softly.

BLOCK 4. Narration: "And intelligence you never have to think about. Every switch speaks to the next, room to room. One home, one gateway."
Shots: a fingertip pressing a glowing switch key / pixel lines of light travelling from that switch through a concrete wall to the next switch / top-down floor plan of an apartment, switches lighting up one after another, linking like tree branches into one small glowing hub / living room curtains gliding closed as a hidden ceiling cove light fades on / the furthest bedroom at the end of a long corridor lighting up instantly.

BLOCK 5. Narration: "And now, light itself. From daylight to candlelight."
Shots: a dining table in crisp cool white daylight / close-up of a ceiling downlight sliding from white to amber / the same table in deep warm candlelight, timber glowing / silhouettes of a family at dinner seen from behind, warm and soft / view through the window of the Singapore skyline at dusk.

BLOCK 6. Narration: "The name was his. What it becomes is mine."
Shots: the older man from behind walking into a beautiful finished living room / his hand reaching out to touch the crystal-covered switch / the room settling into warm golden light / father and son from behind standing side by side / wide shot of the HDB apartment block at night, now with many windows glowing warm.

End on the final wide shot. Leave 4 seconds of quiet at the end for an end card I will add myself.
```
